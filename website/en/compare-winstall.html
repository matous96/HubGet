HubGet vs Winstall.app

Both tools save you from hunting for installers on the web, and both are built on WinGet. Winstall.app is a website that puts together a command for your terminal. HubGet is a desktop app store that installs apps for you, keeps track of their updates and can uninstall them too.

Which one to choose

Each one fits a different job.

HubGet is a good fit if you…

  • want to install, update and uninstall apps without a terminal
  • want an overview of every app on your PC and its updates
  • want to pick from WinGet, Chocolatey and Scoop in one place
  • are setting up a new PC and want it ready with your own bundle of apps in minutes

Winstall.app is a good fit if you…

  • don’t want to install anything and a browser is enough
  • are preparing a command or script for another PC or for automation
  • only use WinGet and are comfortable in a terminal

Questions and answers

Frequently asked questions

Is HubGet a good alternative to Winstall.app?

Yes, if you want to manage apps right in Windows. Winstall.app helps you pick apps and put together a command that you then run in a terminal. HubGet installs the apps itself, shows their progress, keeps track of updates and can uninstall them too – and besides WinGet, its catalog also covers Chocolatey and Scoop.

Can I switch from Winstall.app to HubGet?

Yes. Both tools are built on the same WinGet repository, so the apps from your collections are in the HubGet catalog too. Just search for them and save them to your own package bundle – then install it all at once or export it as a PowerShell script.

Can HubGet install from Chocolatey and Scoop?

Yes. HubGet installs, updates and uninstalls apps from Chocolatey and Scoop right in the app. Both managers also serve as a fallback source when WinGet has a broken download link.

Are both tools free?

Yes. HubGet is open source under the MIT license, free and without ads. Winstall.app is free too, and its source code is published under the GPL-3.0 license.
